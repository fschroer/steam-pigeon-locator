# Steam Pigeon — Requirements

- **Version:** 2.4.4
- **Date:** 2026-08-07
- **Supersedes:** the prose outline in `Steam Pigeon Requirements.docx` (this markdown is now the maintained source).
- **Status key:** each requirement carries a stable ID (`FR-*` functional, `NFR-*` non-functional, `HW-*` hardware). **IDs are append-only, opaque labels — never renumbered.** The historical `FR-P#` numbers embed the priority a requirement was *created* at; as of v2.1 that coupling is retired — the **`Pri` column is the authoritative ranking** (it changes when priorities are reordered) and the **`Status` column** is *Active*, *Deferred*, or *Withdrawn*. A new requirement takes the next free ID regardless of its priority (e.g. `FR-P13` enters at Pri 3).

This document restructures the original outline per issue [#6](https://github.com/fschroer/steam-pigeon-locator/issues/6): traceable IDs, a functional/non-functional split, an explicit definition of the "vetted fusion" gate, and a glossary.

---

## 1. Purpose

Steam Pigeon is a flight-tracking and recovery system for medium- to high-power model rockets. In priority order it must: **(1)** fire the correct recovery charges at the correct moments, **(2)** help the user locate the rocket after landing, and **(3)** record enough data to understand and improve each flight. It comprises three devices: a **Locator** (onboard), a **Receiver** (LoRa↔BLE relay carried by the user), and a phone **App**. Data path: Locator ⇄ (LoRa) ⇄ Receiver ⇄ (BLE) ⇄ App.

## 2. Functional Requirements (priority-ranked)

The original outline's ranked goals were captured as `FR-P1…FR-P12` with the ID number matching priority. As of v2.1 that coupling is retired (see status key): the **`Pri` column is the contract** for resolving trade-offs (P1 highest), IDs are stable labels, and the table is sorted by `Pri` with *Deferred* items last. `FR-P13` (air starts) was inserted at Pri 3; `FR-P16` (arming discipline) at Pri 4; `FR-P14` (connection authorization) at Pri 8; `FR-P15` (interference detection and channel management) at Pri 9.

| ID | Pri | Status | Requirement |
|----|-----|--------|-------------|
| **FR-P1** | 1 | Active | Detect flight-critical events — launch, noseover/apogee (drogue charge timing), and descent altitude (main charge timing) — and fire the corresponding deployment charges. Per the governing policy (§4), these decisions use proven raw sensors, not unvetted fusion. |
| **FR-P2** | 2 | Active | Provide regularly updated latitude/longitude to locate the rocket **after landing** (raw GPS per §4). |
| **FR-P13** | 3 | Active | **Air-start (staged ignition) safety gate.** Detect the conditions required to safely ignite a second-stage/sustainer motor in flight, and **inhibit ignition unless all are met**: booster burnout confirmed, within a configured time-since-launch window, above a minimum altitude, still ascending, and **tilt-from-launch-vertical within a configured limit**. Per §4/NFR-1 the orientation driving the tilt gate must come from a proven source (NFR-9), not unvetted fusion; because ballistic coast offers no gravity reference, elapsed time since the last attitude fix is itself a gate. |
| **FR-P16** | 4 | Active | **Arming discipline: prompt the operator, and keep a disarmed flight diagnosable.** **A forgotten arm fires no charges, so the rocket does not separate, no parachute deploys, and it returns ballistic — destroying the airframe and endangering what is below. Nothing in this requirement prevents that.** The primary control is therefore to make forgetting *less likely*: alert the operator when a prepped rocket stands vertical and disarmed (FR-P6, FR-L7, FR-A10), as a backstop to the pre-flight checklist, which remains the primary procedural control. Secondarily, arming enables the deployment channels and **nothing else** (NFR-12): recording, navigation phase scheduling, and the audible beacon run irrespective of arm state, so the slip does not additionally cost the black box, the position solution, or the means of finding the wreck — this is forensics and recovery, **not** survivability of the flight. Correspondingly, **no sensor-derived condition may enable a deployment channel**; the system prompts rather than arming itself, accepting the ballistic risk as the price of the interlock. From the 2026-08-06 ballistic flight, where one forgotten arm removed four capabilities, only one of which arming exists to control. Rationale, and the analysis rejecting auto-arm, in [ADR-0021](adr/0021-arming-gates-pyro-only.md). |
| **FR-P3** | 5 | Active | Provide device configuration for the locator and receiver. |
| **FR-P4** | 6 | Active | Archive relevant flight data for troubleshooting, fusion-model tuning, flight-dynamics study, sensor evaluation, and design improvement. |
| **FR-P5** | 7 | Active | Provide regularly updated latitude/longitude **during flight**. |
| **FR-P14** | 8 | Active | **Connection authorization.** Gate a user's ability to "connect" to a locator (recognise its telemetry for control and send it commands) behind a per-locator password. The locator is identified by a reasonably-unique ID derived from its MCU UID; the app remembers locators it has been authorized for and challenges for a password only on first contact with an unknown one. Also detect and warn about conflicting traffic (an unrecognised locator) on the current channel. Rationale and mechanism in [ADR-0006](adr/0006-locator-connect-password.md) and [ADR-0020](adr/0020-targeted-locator-commands.md) — **the two halves are separate mechanisms and conflating them was a real defect.** The password decides whether the app will *send*; `target_locator_id` on every command decides which locator *acts* on it. Until ADR-0020 only the first existed, so an honest app's Arm reached every disarmed locator on the channel. |
| **FR-P15** | 9 | Active | **Interference detection and channel management.** Tell the user when the locator↔receiver channel is degraded and why, distinguishing contention from distance, and provide a means to find and move to a clearer channel. A contested channel costs position updates, so this protects delivery of FR-P2 and FR-P5 rather than adding a capability of its own — which is why it sits beside connection authorization: both exist because a launch has many users sharing one band. Rationale, and the correction that co-channel interference *displaces* rather than corrupts (so it is detected by identifying the other locator, not by measuring power), in [ADR-0019](adr/0019-channel-interference-detection.md). |
| **FR-P6** | 10 | Active | Produce locator sound for power-on, arming/disarming, as a post-landing location aid, and to **alert the operator that a prepped rocket is standing vertical while disarmed** (FR-P16, [ADR-0021](adr/0021-arming-gates-pyro-only.md)). The post-landing aid is not gated on arm state. Silence must never be the only indication of a disarmed locator: it is indistinguishable from a flat battery, a failed buzzer, or a locator nobody switched on — which is how a rocket reached the pad disarmed on 2026-08-06. The alert must tolerate a **legally canted rail** (NAR permits 20° from vertical) and the **bobbing of permitted winds** (up to 20 mph sustained) — a gate that goes quiet on a steep, windy launch day fails when it is needed most. It must be suppressible by the operator for a **bounded, self-expiring** period, because a rocket assembled vertically with charges wired is physically indistinguishable from one on the pad; suppression that could be made permanent would be an off switch and is forbidden. |
| **FR-P7** | 11 | Active | Report G-forces on the rocket during flight. |
| **FR-P10** | 12 | Active | Report rocket rotation during flight. |
| **FR-P11** | 13 | Active | Capture general-interest flight data: motor burnout, deployment-channel continuity before and after deployment, and physical parachute-deployment sensing. |
| **FR-P12** | 14 | Active | App text-to-speech for locator status, so the user need not look at the screen. |
| **FR-P8** | — | **Deferred** | Provide fused 3D location. *(Deferred 2026-06-18 per [ADR-0005](adr/0005-retire-ekf-raw-primary.md): the EKF is retired from the real-time path and has no live requirement; raw GPS serves FR-P2/FR-P5.)* |
| **FR-P9** | — | **Deferred** | Provide fused 3D orientation. *(Deferred 2026-06-18 per [ADR-0005](adr/0005-retire-ekf-raw-primary.md): the safety-critical orientation use is now FR-P13 via a high-rate gyro strapdown (NFR-9); the EKF "display" attitude is retired from the real-time path.)* |

### 2.1 Component functional requirements

**Locator**
- FR-L1 — Collect data from the barometric, inertial, and GPS sensors (§HW).
- FR-L2 — Fire deployment charges on four independently configurable channels, each assignable to drogue-primary, drogue-backup, main-primary, main-backup, or left unused. The unused mode excludes a channel from the firing schedule, and is configurable from both the locator's USB-C config menu and the app.
- FR-L3 — Sense deployment-channel continuity before and after firing.
- FR-L4 — Archive flight data to external flash for later download **whether or not the locator is armed**, recording arm state in the record so an absence of deployment events cannot be mistaken for a deployment failure (FR-P16, [ADR-0021](adr/0021-arming-gates-pyro-only.md)). Includes the ~2 s of pre-launch data leading up to launch, timestamped on a GPS-disciplined real-time clock ([ADR-0007](adr/0007-prelaunch-ring-monotonic-clock.md)). Archived data must survive an MCU-only reset (debugger/flash/watchdog) and a flight's committed samples must remain recoverable even if the flight was not cleanly closed (power lost / landing not detected) ([ADR-0010](adr/0010-archive-flash-robustness.md)). The locator must be re-flyable after a landing without a power cycle: a disarm→arm cycle resets the flight state machine and per-flight state and opens a new record ([ADR-0007](adr/0007-prelaunch-ring-monotonic-clock.md) Decision 4, [ADR-0010](adr/0010-archive-flash-robustness.md)).
- FR-L5 — Support configuration and archived-data download over USB-C, plus archive-maintenance commands: reclaim empty/unused records and fully erase the archive (e.g. after a record-structure change) ([ADR-0010](adr/0010-archive-flash-robustness.md)).
- FR-L6 — Broadcast a reasonably-unique locator ID and authenticate its broadcasts with a connection password that can be set **only** over USB-C (never over the air); act only on commands addressed to that ID (FR-P14, [ADR-0006](adr/0006-locator-connect-password.md), [ADR-0020](adr/0020-targeted-locator-commands.md)). **Broadcast arm state explicitly** rather than leaving the app to infer it from which message type arrived, so an armed indication can never stand in for an unarmed flight (FR-P16, NFR-12, [ADR-0021](adr/0021-arming-gates-pyro-only.md) Decision 3).
- FR-L7 — Detect flight events, record, schedule navigation phases, and sound the recovery beacon **irrespective of arm state**; arming gates only the deployment channels (FR-P16, NFR-12, [ADR-0021](adr/0021-arming-gates-pyro-only.md)). Any per-arm calibration the recorded data depends on — notably the mounting frame — must therefore also be established without an arm event, or a disarmed flight is recorded through the wrong axes. Because verticality cannot be detected without already knowing the mounting frame, the **nose axis is configured** rather than inferred ([ADR-0021](adr/0021-arming-gates-pyro-only.md) Decision 6 amendment), and must be settable from both the app and USB-C (as FR-L2). Alert the operator, audibly and independently of the app, when the locator is disarmed while a prepped rocket stands vertical (FR-P6); the alert is gated on evidence of a live rocket (sustained verticality, rotational quiescence, and deployment-channel continuity) and must not degrade into a timer the operator learns to ignore.

**Receiver**
- FR-R1 — Relay messages between the locator and the app.
- FR-R2 — Provide receiver-specific information and status to the app, answering a receiver-info request on its own behalf **regardless of locator activity** (the app relies on this to keep the BLE link alive during locator silence — NFR-11, [ADR-0012](adr/0012-app-ble-connection-health-probe.md)).
- FR-R3 — Indicate charging, BLE connectivity, and communication status via LEDs.
- FR-R4 — Support configuration over USB-C.
- FR-R6 — Measure link quality on every relayed broadcast (SNR, idle-channel noise floor, count of frames that failed to decode) and, on request while the locator is disarmed, survey the band and report per-channel occupancy including frames decoded on each candidate (FR-P15, [ADR-0019](adr/0019-channel-interference-detection.md)).
- FR-R5 — Follow the locator's LoRa channel when relaying a locator channel change, switching only after the forwarded command has finished transmitting, so the locator↔receiver link is preserved without manual reconfiguration ([ADR-0011](adr/0011-locator-lora-channel-from-app.md)).

**App**
- FR-A1 — Display pre-flight and in-flight telemetry.
- FR-A2 — Display rocket location relative to the user's phone.
- FR-A3 — Speak important locator status (FR-P12).
- FR-A4 — Allow the user to update locator and receiver configuration. This includes the LoRa channel via two distinct controls ([ADR-0011](adr/0011-locator-lora-channel-from-app.md)): changing a *locator's* channel (the receiver follows automatically to keep the link) and changing the *receiver's* channel independently (to switch to a different locator already on another channel). A failed locator channel change reverts the receiver to the prior channel and retries.
- FR-A5 — Provide a heads-up view: rocket orientation, key telemetry in graphical form, and an aid to locating the rocket in the sky during flight.
- FR-A6 — Display archived flight information graphically (downloaded over LoRa via the reliable windowed/parity transfer — [ADR-0009](adr/0009-flight-data-transfer-reliability.md)).
- FR-A7 — Provide a means to remotely test deployment charges.
- FR-A9 — Classify the link from the receiver's measurements and report interference distinctly from distance, staying silent when a weak, noisy signal is explained by range; offer a ranked clean-channel choice from a band survey, never offering a channel with a locator decoded on it, and move the locator (receiver following) rather than only re-pointing the receiver (FR-P15, [ADR-0019](adr/0019-channel-interference-detection.md), [ADR-0011](adr/0011-locator-lora-channel-from-app.md)).
- FR-A10 — Take arm state from the locator's explicit broadcast flag and **never** from which message type arrived; on an absent or unparseable flag, report disarmed/unknown rather than falling back to inference. Alert the operator — by voice (FR-A3) and by a persistent visual state — when a prepped rocket stands vertical while disarmed, latched per transition and escalating rather than repeating on a fixed timer. Offer a **bounded, self-expiring** suppression of that alert, available only while it is actually sounding, and show the suppressed state distinctly from "no alert" — a silenced system that looks identical to a healthy one is the failure FR-P16 exists to prevent (FR-P16, [ADR-0021](adr/0021-arming-gates-pyro-only.md) Decision 5 as amended).
- FR-A8 — Recognise only authorized locators: challenge for a password on first contact with an unknown locator, remember authorized locators, warn about conflicting traffic on the current channel, and address every locator-directed command to the connected locator (FR-P14, [ADR-0006](adr/0006-locator-connect-password.md), [ADR-0020](adr/0020-targeted-locator-commands.md)).

## 4. Governing policy: proven sensors over unvetted fusion

**NFR-1 (policy).** For **FR-P1** (deployment-critical altitude and velocity), **FR-P2** (post-landing location), and **FR-P13** (air-start tilt-safety gate), known sensor capabilities take precedence over unproven sensor-fusion outputs. Specifically, deployment gating uses **raw barometric** altitude and velocity, post-landing location uses **raw GPS**, and the air-start tilt gate uses the **high-rate gyro strapdown** (NFR-9) — never the unvetted EKF — until and unless a fused quantity passes the vetting gate (NFR-2). Velocity used for FR-P1 must come from a proven source.

**NFR-2 (vetted-fusion gate).** A fused quantity may replace a raw source for an FR-P1/P2 use only after it is validated against an **independent reference** (not against the raw source it would replace) per **[ADR-0004](adr/0004-fusion-vetting-method.md)**, and the relevant ADR is updated to Accepted for that specific use. The current deployment-source policy is fixed by **[ADR-0003](adr/0003-priority1-deployment-raw-baro.md)** (raw baro is the permanent FR-P1 primary; fusion is a robustness layer only).

> Rationale and the full decision history live in the ADRs; this requirement is the single normative statement the firmware must satisfy.

## 5. Non-Functional Requirements

| ID | Requirement |
|----|-------------|
| **NFR-3 (real-time)** | All per-cycle code must complete within the 50 ms window of a 20 Hz processing loop. Design target ≈ 25 ms to leave margin for jitter and future functionality. |
| **NFR-4 (ISR discipline)** | Interrupt service routines must exit as soon as possible, queueing any blocking work for normal (main-loop) execution. |
| **NFR-5 (bus contention)** | The barometric sensor, IMU, and external flash share a common SPI bus; the GPS is on a separate I2C bus. Traffic on the shared SPI bus must be serialized to avoid conflicting transactions. *(Corrected per [#3](https://github.com/fschroer/steam-pigeon-locator/issues/3); the original outline incorrectly listed the GPS on the SPI bus.)* |
| **NFR-6 (baro filtering)** | The MS5611 has no hardware filter to reject spurious values, so spike rejection must be implemented in software. |
| **NFR-7 (baro timing)** | The MS5611 requires careful sequencing of its D1 and D2 conversions; the locator must guarantee a valid pressure value is available in every 50 ms window. |
| **NFR-8 (continuity safety)** | Deployment-channel continuity sensing must not energize a charge. |
| **NFR-9 (high-rate attitude)** | Orientation used for safety-critical gating (FR-P13) must be produced by a **high-rate strapdown gyro integrator** (target ≥ 480 Hz, decoupled from the 20 Hz loop of NFR-3 and respecting NFR-4 ISR discipline), seeded from the known on-pad vertical attitude; it must not depend on unvetted fusion (NFR-1). The gyro full-scale range must exceed the maximum expected body rate (flight rates reach ~768 dps; the ISM6HG256X is configured at ±4000 dps). Because ballistic coast provides no gravity reference, the estimator dead-reckons through coast and its confidence degrades with elapsed time since the last attitude fix. |
| **NFR-10 (reliability / fault recovery)** | The locator must automatically recover from a firmware hang and preserve enough state across an unexpected reset to diagnose it. A hardware **independent watchdog (IWDG)**, refreshed once per super-loop cycle from main-loop context (respecting NFR-4), resets the MCU if the loop stalls; a persistent **fault/hang record** — CPU exception frame + fault-status registers on a fault, last checkpoint tag + uptime on a watchdog hang, reset cause + boot count on a normal boot — survives the reset in non-initialized (`.noinit`) RAM for later readout. Mechanism in [ADR-0008](adr/0008-watchdog-fault-log.md). |
| **NFR-12 (arming interlock)** | A deployment channel may be energized **only** after an explicit operator arm command addressed to that locator (FR-P14, [ADR-0020](adr/0020-targeted-locator-commands.md)). No sensor-derived condition — accelerometer, barometer, GPS, timer, or any combination — may enable a deployment channel, and no future robustness feature may relax this. Conversely, arming must gate **nothing but** the deployment channels: coupling recording, navigation, or the recovery beacon to it makes one operator slip remove capabilities that the interlock does not exist to control. Sibling to NFR-8 (continuity sensing must not energize a charge); rationale and the rejected auto-arm design in [ADR-0021](adr/0021-arming-gates-pyro-only.md). |
| **NFR-11 (app link resilience)** | The app must keep its BLE connection to the receiver alive through arbitrary periods of locator silence (locator powered off, on the pad, or out of LoRa range) — an idle link must not be torn down. Because Android can report a dead (OS-cached "phantom") GATT link as connected, the app verifies liveness by **probing the receiver** (a receiver-info request the receiver answers on its own behalf), reconnecting only after repeated probes go unanswered, rather than inferring a dead link from the absence of relayed locator data. Mechanism in [ADR-0012](adr/0012-app-ble-connection-health-probe.md). |

## 6. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **AGL** | Altitude Above Ground Level — height relative to the launch pad (or current terrain), as opposed to MSL (mean sea level). |
| **Apogee** | The highest point of the trajectory, where vertical velocity passes through zero. |
| **Noseover** | The pitch-over at/near apogee as the rocket transitions from ascent to descent; used here as the event that starts drogue-deployment timing. |
| **Drogue** | The small parachute deployed at/near apogee to stabilize descent before the main. |
| **Main** | The large parachute deployed at low altitude for a soft landing. |
| **Primary / Backup** | Each deployment role has a primary charge (fires first, at the nominal trigger) and a backup charge (fires on a delay or lower altitude as redundancy). |
| **ZUPT** | Zero-velocity UPdaTe — a navigation correction that constrains velocity to zero while the rocket is known to be stationary on the pad. |
| **Mounting calibration** | Establishes the body←sensor frame used to remap IMU output into the standard body frame (+X = nose). Where the **nose axis** is configured the frame follows from it directly; where it is left `Auto` the frame is detected from the gravity axis on each arm, as before. Also retriggered on a pad settle, which re-seeds the strapdown at the pad orientation (FR-L7, [ADR-0021](adr/0021-arming-gates-pyro-only.md) Decision 6 as amended). |
| **Nose axis** | Which raw sensor axis the rocket's long axis lies **along** — a static property of how the locator is mounted, and therefore configuration. Stating it is what makes tilt-from-vertical measurable off the pad: detection alone cannot distinguish a vertical rocket from one lying on its side, since both put gravity along a cardinal axis. **Unsigned** — which way up the locator is bolted is measured from gravity, not configured, and both polarities count as vertical ([ADR-0021](adr/0021-arming-gates-pyro-only.md) Decision 6 amendment). |
| **Armed / Disarmed** | The locator state that enables or disables the deployment channels — and, per NFR-12, nothing else. A disarmed locator still records, navigates, broadcasts, and beacons; it simply cannot fire a charge. |
| **Ballistic** | A flight whose recovery devices never deployed: the rocket does not separate, no parachute opens, and it descends nose-first under gravity as an unpowered missile — destroying the airframe and endangering anything beneath it. The outcome a forgotten arm produced on 2026-08-06. FR-P16 exists to make it **less likely**; nothing in the system makes it less damaging once it happens. |
| **Fused / fusion** | An estimate produced by combining multiple sensors (e.g. the INS EKF blending IMU, baro, GPS), as opposed to a single raw sensor reading. |
| **Raw** | A single sensor's direct measurement (here, with software spike rejection), trusted for FR-P1/P2 per NFR-1. |
| **Vetting gate** | The objective criteria (NFR-2 / ADR-0004) a fused quantity must pass before it may be trusted for a deployment-critical or post-landing use. |
| **Air start / staging** | Igniting a second-stage or sustainer motor in flight, after the booster. Requires a safety gate (FR-P13) so the motor lights only when the rocket is pointed safely. |
| **Strapdown / high-rate attitude** | Body-frame orientation obtained by integrating the gyro at a high rate (NFR-9), as opposed to an estimator running at the 20 Hz loop rate. |
| **Tilt-from-vertical** | The angle between the rocket's thrust axis and launch-vertical (up) — the quantity FR-P13 gates on. Distinct from compass heading, which a 6-axis IMU cannot observe at rest. |
| **Locator ID** | A reasonably-unique identifier for a locator, derived from its STM32 MCU UID, broadcast in the clear so the app can identify (but not yet authenticate) which locator it is hearing (FR-P14). |
| **Connection authorization** | The app's gate on which locators it will recognise and command, enforced by a per-locator password. A password-seeded checksum (`auth_tag`) on the locator's PreLaunchData broadcast lets the app verify it holds the correct password without a round-trip; an "open" locator has no password set. (FR-P14 / [ADR-0006](adr/0006-locator-connect-password.md).) |

## 7. Change log

| Version | Date | Change |
|---------|------|--------|
| 2.4.4 | 2026-08-07 | **FR-P6 and FR-A10 extended: the disarmed alert must survive real launch conditions, and must be suppressible.** Two of [ADR-0021](adr/0021-arming-gates-pyro-only.md) Decision 5's stated thresholds were wrong in the same direction — too tight, so the alert would have **gone silent exactly when needed**. (a) The ~20° verticality gate equalled the NAR-permitted rail angle, so a legally canted rocket sat on the boundary and any measurement error pushed it outside. Now 35°. (b) Requiring rotational quiescence meant a rocket bobbing on a rod in the 20 mph winds launches are permitted in would never satisfy the gate; stationarity is dropped from the alert (Decision 6's mounting calibration keeps it — that one must trust the gravity vector) and the settle counter is leaky instead. Separately, a **bounded auto-expiring snooze** is added, because a rocket assembled vertically with charges wired is physically identical to one on the pad and no sensor separates them — without an escape hatch the buzzer gets taped over, removing the warning entirely. The snooze is clamped by the locator, cleared by a power cycle, does not pause the underlying detector, and is reported distinctly so a suppressed system never looks like a healthy one. Thresholds remain **unvalidated starting points**. Issue [#37](https://github.com/fschroer/steam-pigeon-locator/issues/37). |
| 2.4.3 | 2026-08-07 | **Glossary *Nose axis* corrected — the setting is unsigned; no requirement text change.** The first implementation asked for a signed direction (±X, ±Y, ±Z), which required the operator to know which way up the locator is bolted. The circularity that motivated the setting is in the **axis**, not the sign: a rocket lying flat has gravity along a cardinal axis just as a standing one does, so the axis is unrecoverable — but once stated, the sign follows from one accelerometer reading, and calibration only ever commits with the rocket vertical, where that component is a full ±1 g. So the axis is configured and the sign measured; both polarities count as vertical for the FR-P6/FR-A10 alert. [ADR-0021](adr/0021-arming-gates-pyro-only.md) Decision 6's amendment carries the reasoning. **Note for anyone flashing:** unlike the other changes in this run, this one does *not* change any struct size, so nothing on the wire can detect a mismatched component — a stale app or receiver selects the **wrong axis silently**. Flash all three together. |
| 2.4.2 | 2026-08-07 | **FR-P16 retitled and reframed; no scope or priority change.** The requirement was titled "arming discipline and disarmed-flight **survivability**", and said a forgotten arm "costs the flight its recovery deployment but not its black box". **Both overstate what the system does.** A locator that fires no charges means the rocket never separates and no parachute deploys: it returns nose-first under gravity as an unpowered missile, destroying the airframe and endangering what is below. Recording that faithfully is forensics, not mitigation — the flight does not survive, and **nothing in FR-P16, NFR-12, FR-L7 or FR-A10 reduces the energy arriving at the ground**. The requirement now says so, and orders its two halves by weight: making forgetting *less likely* (the alert, backstopping the pre-flight checklist) is primary; keeping the aftermath diagnosable is secondary. Glossary *Ballistic* rewritten to state the physical outcome.
